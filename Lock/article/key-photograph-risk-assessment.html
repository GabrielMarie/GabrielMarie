Reconstructing a Key from a Photograph: A Security Risk Assessment

A key does not have to be lost or stolen for the access it provides to be compromised. A photograph may reveal enough information to support its reconstruction, even when the original key never leaves its owner’s possession.

In my paper, Security Risk Assessment: Reconstructing a Key from a Low- Quality Photograph with Help from AI, I examine this risk and the role artificial intelligence can play in making existing methods more accessible. AI is not the source of the vulnerability, but it can help lower the technical barriers involved in preparing the tools used to analyze a key photograph.

The paper also considers why master-key systems deserve particular attention. When one key provides access to many rooms, its exposure can have consequences far beyond a single door. The scope of that access matters just as much as the circumstances in which the key becomes visible.

Beyond the technical discussion, I examine everyday key handling, the assignment of access privileges, and the responsibilities placed on employees who carry highly privileged keys. My concern is not simply whether an employee makes a mistake, but whether the organization provides the training, practical procedures, and support needed to protect the access it has granted.

The document brings these technical and organizational questions together as a security risk assessment. It is intended to encourage a closer look at what keys reveal, who carries them, and how their exposure is managed.
